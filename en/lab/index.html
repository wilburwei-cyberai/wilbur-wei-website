THE LAB

Turn attack and defence scenarios into testable questions.

MIRAGE Lab is based in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Yuan Ze University. Assistant Professor Wilbur Wei (Te-En Wei) is its Director. Its research focuses on AI agent security, red teaming and zero trust, examining how attack scenarios, authorisation boundaries and behavioural evidence inform the design and evaluation of defences. Future directions include drone and robot communication security and PUF hardware security applications.

Third place, 21st Cybersecurity Skills Golden Shield Award

Team: Nullptrtracefishdeleted

Nullptrtracefishdeleted, a student team from MIRAGE Lab in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Yuan Ze University (Director: Assistant Professor Wilbur Wei), won third place in the university and college division (大專校院組) of the 21st Cybersecurity Skills Golden Shield Award (資安技能金盾獎) in 2026.

The Cybersecurity Skills Golden Shield Award is organised by the Administration for Cyber Security, Ministry of Digital Affairs (moda), and run by the National Institute of Cyber Security, with guidance from the Ministry of Digital Affairs and the Ministry of Education. The 21st edition drew 224 registered teams, 40 of which reached the final, held on 27 November 2026 in a Capture The Flag (CTF) format.

Competition details: Administration for Cyber Security press release (Chinese) · Official competition rules, 21st edition (Chinese)

FUTURE RESEARCH

Next: communication and hardware security

Building on existing AI security and zero-trust research, I plan to deepen work in communication security and hardware security. The directions below are prospective research plans, open to discussion on problem definition, experimental design and industry collaboration.

Planned research

Communication security | Drones and robots

How can devices, controllers and services establish trust in commands and data?

Planned work examines authentication, command authorisation and communication protection between drones, robots and control systems. Threat scenarios such as impersonation, replay and unauthorised control will inform evaluation of defences and their effects on latency, availability and operational safety.

Planned research

Hardware security | PUF applications

How can hardware characteristics support device identity and key protection?

Explore Physical Unclonable Functions (PUFs) for device identification, authentication and key generation. Planned evaluation considers reliability under environmental variation, resource constraints and resistance to attacks, as well as connections to device and communication security.

These are prospective directions. Published papers and awards are listed separately.
