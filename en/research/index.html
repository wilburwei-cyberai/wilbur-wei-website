FUTURE RESEARCH

Next: communication and hardware security

Building on existing AI security and zero-trust research, I plan to deepen work in communication security and hardware security. The directions below are prospective research plans, open to discussion on problem definition, experimental design and industry collaboration.

Planned research

Communication security | Drones and robots

How can devices, controllers and services establish trust in commands and data?

Planned work examines authentication, command authorisation and communication protection between drones, robots and control systems. Threat scenarios such as impersonation, replay and unauthorised control will inform evaluation of defences and their effects on latency, availability and operational safety.

Planned research

Hardware security | PUF applications

How can hardware characteristics support device identity and key protection?

Explore Physical Unclonable Functions (PUFs) for device identification, authentication and key generation. Planned evaluation considers reliability under environmental variation, resource constraints and resistance to attacks, as well as connections to device and communication security.

These are prospective directions. Published papers and awards are listed separately.

2025 / STIE-ZTA

STIE-ZTA: Self-adaptive Trust Inference Engine for Policy-based Zero Trust Architecture

Te-En Wei、Jung-Kai Lin、Ting-Chen Hu、Chia-Hai Cheng、Szu-Ying Lee、Jen-Fu Wang

35th Cryptology and Information Security Conference (CISC 2025) · Honorable Mention

Zero-trust architecture requires continuous verification of every subject that requests access. The practical difficulty is that enterprises already run many security tools — intrusion detection, firewalls, endpoint protection — each producing its own data, with no unified way to combine them into a judgement about whether a subject can be trusted. STIE-ZTA is a policy-based, self-adaptive trust inference framework that aggregates and analyses this heterogeneous data to evaluate each subject's behavioural trust level continuously, so that organisations can make timelier and better-grounded access decisions while getting more out of the infrastructure they already have.
